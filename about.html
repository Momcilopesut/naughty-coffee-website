The roastery

Coffee, from seed to cup.

Nori started with a second-hand roaster in the back of a breakfast kitchen. It still lives there — in full view of the counter, smelling of toast and caramelising sugar.

Why “Nori”?

Nori is the thin sheet of seaweed that holds a whole meal together without getting in the way. That's what we want our coffee to do for breakfast — and what we want breakfast to do for your morning.

The menu leans Japanese, the coffee leans light, and everything is made in one room.

How we work

Four steps, no shortcuts.

  1. Source

    We cup samples from importers who work directly with producers, and pay well above commodity price for the lots we choose.

  2. Roast

    Small 12kg batches with a profile for each coffee, logged and adjusted every week.

  3. Rest

    Fresh isn't best on day one. Every batch rests so its flavours settle before it's served.

  4. Brew

    Our baristas taste every morning and dial in recipes for each coffee on the bar.

Our story

A few milestones.

  1. Year one

    A kitchen with a roaster in it

    We open as a breakfast spot, roasting two coffees on a five-kilo machine in the corner.

  2. Year two

    Beans go online

    Regulars kept asking to take coffee home, so we started shipping it.

  3. Year three

    A bigger drum

    A 12kg roaster replaces the original — the old one now sits on the retail shelf as a plant stand.

  4. Today

    Six origins, one room

    Coffee bar, roastery, kitchen and retail shelf of tees and hats, all under one roof.

Visit

Come for coffee. Stay for eggs.

Walk-ins only, all day. The roaster runs Monday and Thursday mornings — grab a stool by the window and watch a batch drop.
