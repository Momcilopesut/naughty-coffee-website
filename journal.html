Notes from
the bar.

Short reads on brewing, roasting and the things customers ask us most.

(COFFEE_FRESHNESS / 01)

Is fresher always better?

Freshly roasted coffee is full of carbon dioxide. Brew it on day one and that gas pushes water away from the grounds, giving a sharp, hollow cup.

So we rest every batch. Filter coffees taste best from about day 5 to day 30 after roasting, espresso from day 10. The roast date is printed on every bag, so you can plan your next order around it.

(POUR_OVER / 02)

Five steps to a better pour over

Use 15g of coffee to 250g of water, ground about as fine as table salt. Rinse the paper, then pour just enough water to wet all the grounds and wait 40 seconds while it blooms.

Pour the rest in slow circles, in two or three stages, and aim to finish around three minutes. If it tastes sour, grind finer; if it tastes bitter, grind coarser. Change one thing at a time.

(LIGHT_ROAST / 03)

Why we roast light

Roasting darker makes every coffee taste more alike: toasty, bitter, heavy. Roasting lighter keeps what makes each origin different, from Ethiopian jasmine to Kenyan blackcurrant.

Light doesn't mean sour. We stretch the time after first crack just enough to develop sweetness, then stop before the roast takes over the flavour of the bean.

(MATCHA_MANIA / 04)

Matcha: what to look for

Good matcha is bright, almost electric green and smells sweet and grassy. Dull, olive-coloured powder is usually old or low grade, and it will taste bitter however you make it.

Sift it before whisking so it doesn't clump, use water around 75°C rather than boiling, and whisk in a quick zig-zag until a fine foam forms. We serve ours over oat milk with a little honey.
